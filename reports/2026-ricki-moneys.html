Report

Physical

Strong, filled-in frame at 6'0", 180, already thicker through the lower half than a typical shortstop, and he only turned 18 on Oct. 9, a little over a month ago. He played the whole 2026 season at 17. There is still room to add strength; the key is keeping his athleticism as he does.

Hitting

The approach is the standout for a debut season: more walks than strikeouts (46 to 45) and a .440 OBP at 17. He doesn't expand the zone much and lets pitchers come to him. The open question is pure bat-to-ball. Scouts have flagged swing-and-miss risk, and DSL pitching rarely tests a hitter with quality spin. How the contact holds up against better breaking balls stateside is the key to the whole profile.

Power

The carrying tool. Electric bat speed and natural power to right-center. He reached 111 mph exit velocity, with a 90th-percentile exit velocity above 106 mph, which the Brewers say is the best among their DSL hitters since 2017, along with his hard-hit and barrel rates. That is elite exit velocity for a 17-year-old, and it is why the future grade is 65. Game power is still catching up (5 HR, 16 doubles and triples), but with his age and strength, 30-homer power is a realistic outcome as he adds loft.

Speed / baserunning

Average runner today who plays faster than his times: 28 steals in 33 attempts (85%) in 53 games. He reads pitchers well and gets good jumps, and as he gets stronger and more efficient in his stride he projects as an above-average runner who adds value on the bases. DSL catchers inflate steal totals, so the success rate is the better signal.

Defense

Good hands and a strong arm, but the range and first step fall short of an everyday shortstop. Third base is the most likely landing spot: the arm and hands fit, and the work there is consistency on routine plays. Second base is a fallback if he stays lean. Wherever he ends up, the bat will need to carry him.

Makeup

Handled the jump from the international showcase circuit to pro ball without trouble, playing his entire debut season at 17. Won the club's DSL Player of the Month award in June and kept producing over the full summer, finishing with more walks than strikeouts.

Development

The 2027 move to the Arizona Complex League or Single-A Carolina is the real test. The things to watch: whether the walk rate survives better pitching, his strikeout rate against spin, and whether he keeps working at shortstop or moves to third full time.

Summary

Moneys has some of the loudest raw power in the Brewers' system and backed it up with a mature approach in his pro debut, posting a .919 OPS with more walks than strikeouts and 28 steals, all before he turned 18 in October. The DSL sample comes with the usual caveats, and the hit tool against better spin is unproven. A move to third base looks likely, which puts more weight on the bat. If the contact holds as he climbs, 65 power, real patience and above-average speed make him a solid everyday third baseman who hits in the middle of the order. He is one of the highest-upside bats in the lower levels of the system.
